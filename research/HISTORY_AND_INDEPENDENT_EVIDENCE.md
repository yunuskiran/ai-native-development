## Prior framework comparison, reviewed beyond the abstract

The Macedo preprint's method/rubric/limitations were inspected, not only its abstract ([W04](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-w04), R-C01). It is a documentary, purposefully selected comparison with a traction filter, single-rater scoring and a declared author relationship to Reversa. Its scores are judgments, not measured framework effectiveness. We do not import them. Its older GSD portability account cannot be applied to the currently documented Core host integrations without a snapshot reconciliation. The HTML's v1 submission date and internal heading date differ; preserve that bibliographic ambiguity.

This is useful prior work and prevents a “first framework comparison” marketing claim. It neither validates our instrument nor supplies missing outcomes for our four arms. Our F01–F11 questions come from the accepted master/architecture; any conceptual borrowing must be attributed.

## Controlled evidence about AI-assisted work, not these four methods

METR's 2025 primary report describes 16 experienced developers and 246 issues, with a reported 19% time increase under AI-allowed conditions ([W05](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-w05), R-C02). This is a bounded counterexample to inevitable acceleration, not a BMAD/GSD/AI-DLC/AIUP trial. Tool era, repository familiarity, tasks and quality expectations limit transfer; raw data were not reanalyzed here.

METR's 2026 update reports severe participation/task-selection and concurrent-agent timing problems ([W06](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-w06), R-C03). It explicitly cautions against treating its later estimate as a clean productivity signal. Neither publication settles a universal speedup/slowdown or ranks the four book frameworks.

## Field accounts and documentary critique

An AI-DLC collaborative-platform account reports verification limits and a concrete empty-success incident; its author bio identifies AWS affiliation ([W07](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-w07), R-C04). Treat it as a disclosed-interest first-hand account about a particular sample, not independent method-superiority evidence. Its reported timings and teammate records are not our benchmark results.

A third-party AI-DLC source review distinguishes the method, workflow implementation and sample platforms, with an August snapshot ([R05](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-r05), R-C05). It describes itself as independent, but no controlled trial is established by that description. Its historical implementation numbers do not replace inspected October source defaults.

## Search limits and gaps

Directed queries covered BMAD/GSD controlled methodology evaluations, AIUP empirical evidence and AI-DLC evaluation/limitations, plus the original historical and technical sources. This is not a database-wide systematic search. No controlled, current head-to-head comparison of all four frameworks was established within the inspected set. That is a limit of this search, not proof that no such evidence exists.

A Tessl evaluation listing surfaced, but direct retrieval failed ([W15](M2_SOURCE_LEDGER.md#m2-w15)). Its search-result metrics were not accepted as findings. Artifact-grading evals, if later inspected, must still be distinguished from complete-process, team or production outcomes. Full comparative/book text access and independent technical review remain separate requirements.

## Consequences for the book and later case

Present mechanisms as documented; outcomes as observed only under stated conditions. Plan a tiny-change counterexample, retain failed runs and separate active-human effort, waiting, agent work and total adoption cost. A realistic SaaS story can illuminate process but cannot replace controlled or longitudinal evidence. C09 must retain unassessed outcomes; C14 must label future scenarios rather than extrapolate a capability curve from dated studies.
